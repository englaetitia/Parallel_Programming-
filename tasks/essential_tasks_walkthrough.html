The one tool: Taylor series

For a smooth ff, the value at x+hx+h is built from the derivatives at xx: f(x+h)=∑k=0∞hkk!f(k)(x).f(x+h)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{h^k}{k!}\,f^{(k)}(x). For x−hx-h, replace hh by −h-h. Then hkh^k becomes (−h)k=(−1)khk(-h)^k=(-1)^kh^k, so the terms with odd kk change sign and the even ones do not. That is why the two expansions differ only in the signs of the f′f' and f‴f''' terms.

Every Task below adds, subtracts or combines these expansions so that the unwanted terms cancel.

Task 3.2: the second derivative

Goal: a formula for f″(x)f''(x) using only f(x−h)f(x-h), f(x)f(x) and f(x+h)f(x+h).

Step 1. Write both expansions up to h4h^4: f(x+h)=f+hf′+h22f″+h36f‴+h424f⁗+…f(x+h)=f+hf'+\frac{h^2}{2}f''+\frac{h^3}{6}f'''+\frac{h^4}{24}f''''+\dots f(x−h)=f−hf′+h22f″−h36f‴+h424f⁗+…f(x-h)=f-hf'+\frac{h^2}{2}f''-\frac{h^3}{6}f'''+\frac{h^4}{24}f''''+\dots

Step 2. Add them term by term:

  • f+f=2ff+f=2f
  • hf′−hf′=0hf'-hf'=0
  • h22f″+h22f″=h2f″\frac{h^2}{2}f''+\frac{h^2}{2}f''=h^2f''
  • h36f‴−h36f‴=0\frac{h^3}{6}f'''-\frac{h^3}{6}f'''=0
  • h424f⁗+h424f⁗=h412f⁗\frac{h^4}{24}f''''+\frac{h^4}{24}f''''=\frac{h^4}{12}f''''
  • the h5h^5 terms are odd and cancel too, so the next term is of order h6h^6.

f(x+h)+f(x−h)=2f+h2f″+h412f⁗+𝒪(h6).f(x+h)+f(x-h)=2f+h^2f''+\frac{h^4}{12}f''''+\mathcal O(h^6).

Step 3. Subtract 2f2f from both sides and divide by h2h^2: D2f(x)=f(x−h)−2f(x)+f(x+h)h2=f″(x)+h212f⁗(x)+𝒪(h4)\boxed{\,D_2f(x)=\frac{f(x-h)-2f(x)+f(x+h)}{h^2}=f''(x)+\frac{h^2}{12}f''''(x)+\mathcal O(h^4)\,} The error is proportional to h2h^2, so the formula is second-order accurate.

Second route (undetermined coefficients). Look for af(x−h)+bf(x)+cf(x+h)h2\dfrac{a\,f(x-h)+b\,f(x)+c\,f(x+h)}{h^2}. Expanding each term gives a+b+ch2f+c−ahf′+a+c2f″+…\frac{a+b+c}{h^2}\,f+\frac{c-a}{h}\,f'+\frac{a+c}{2}\,f''+\dots To obtain exactly f″f'' we need a+b+c=0,c−a=0,a+c2=1.a+b+c=0,\qquad c-a=0,\qquad \frac{a+c}{2}=1. The second equation gives a=ca=c, the third then gives a=c=1a=c=1, and the first gives b=−2b=-2. Same formula.

Step 4 (part c): numerical check with f=sinf=\sin at x=1x=1. The exact values are f″(1)=−sin⁡1=−0.841471,f⁗(1)=sin⁡1=0.841471,f''(1)=-\sin\,1=-0.841471,\qquad f''''(1)=\sin\,1=0.841471, so the predicted error is h212sin⁡1\dfrac{h^2}{12}\sin\,1.

Sanity checks (Task 3.3). For f = 1: (1 − 2 + 1)/h² = 0. For f = x: ((x − h) − 2x + (x + h))/h² = 0. Both are exact, as they must be: a constant and a straight line have zero second derivative, and the error term (h²/12)f⁗ vanishes for any polynomial of degree ≤ 3, so any reasonable approximation must reproduce them exactly. The coefficients add up to 1−2+1=01-2+1=0, because a constant has zero second derivative. For f=x2f=x^2: (x−h)2−2x2+(x+h)2h2=2h2h2=2,\frac{(x-h)^2-2x^2+(x+h)^2}{h^2}=\frac{2h^2}{h^2}=2, the exact answer.

Task 3.4: the discrete Laplacian

Step 1. Freeze y=yjy=y_j and apply Task 3.2 along xx with spacing Δx\Delta x: ∂2u∂x2≈ui−1,j−2ui,j+ui+1,jΔx2.\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}\approx\frac{u_{i-1,j}-2u_{i,j}+u_{i+1,j}}{\Delta x^2}.

Step 2. Freeze x=xix=x_i and apply it along yy: ∂2u∂y2≈ui,j−1−2ui,j+ui,j+1Δy2.\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial y^2}\approx\frac{u_{i,j-1}-2u_{i,j}+u_{i,j+1}}{\Delta y^2}.

Step 3. Add them. For Δx=Δy=h\Delta x=\Delta y=h the two −2ui,j-2u_{i,j} terms combine to −4ui,j-4u_{i,j}: ∇h2ui,j=ui−1,j+ui+1,j+ui,j−1+ui,j+1−4ui,jh2\boxed{\,\nabla_h^2u_{i,j}=\frac{u_{i-1,j}+u_{i+1,j}+u_{i,j-1}+u_{i,j+1}-4u_{i,j}}{h^2}\,}

Error. Each direction has error Δ212\frac{\Delta^2}{12} times a fourth derivative, so ∇h2u−∇2u=Δx212uxxxx+Δy212uyyyy+…,\nabla_h^2u-\nabla^2u=\frac{\Delta x^2}{12}u_{xxxx}+\frac{\Delta y^2}{12}u_{yyyy}+\dots, second order in both spacings.

Stencil. The weights, to be divided by h2h^2: +1+1−4+1+1\begin{array}{ccc} & +1 & \\ +1 & -4 & +1\\ & +1 & \end{array} The diagonal neighbours have weight 00. This is why the parallel code does not need corner ghost values.

Part (f), the meaning. Let u‾i,j=14(ui−1,j+ui+1,j+ui,j−1+ui,j+1)\bar u_{i,j}=\tfrac14\left(u_{i-1,j}+u_{i+1,j}+u_{i,j-1}+u_{i,j+1}\right) be the mean of the four neighbours. The numerator equals 4u‾i,j−4ui,j4\bar u_{i,j}-4u_{i,j}, so ∇h2ui,j=4h2(u‾i,j−ui,j).\nabla_h^2u_{i,j}=\frac{4}{h^2}\left(\bar u_{i,j}-u_{i,j}\right). The mean-value formula of Section 1.4 says that the average of uu over a circle of radius ρ\rho is u+ρ24∇2u+𝒪(ρ4)u+\frac{\rho^2}{4}\nabla^2u+\mathcal O(\rho^4), i.e. ∇2u≈4ρ2(average−u)\nabla^2u\approx\frac{4}{\rho^2}(\text{average}-u). Here the four neighbours are four equally spaced points of a circle of radius hh, so it is the same statement.

Task 5.2: the update rule

Step 1 (method of lines). Replace the space derivatives and keep time continuous. At each interior node dui,jdt=α∇h2ui,j,\frac{du_{i,j}}{dt}=\alpha\,\nabla_h^2u_{i,j}, one ODE per node.

Step 2 (forward Euler). For an ODE dudt=F\dfrac{du}{dt}=F, forward Euler says un+1=un+ΔtFu^{n+1}=u^n+\Delta t\,F. Here F=α∇h2unF=\alpha\nabla_h^2u^n: ui,jn+1=ui,jn+αΔth2(ui+1,jn+ui−1,jn+ui,j+1n+ui,j−1n−4ui,jn).u^{n+1}_{i,j}=u^n_{i,j}+\frac{\alpha\,\Delta t}{h^2}\left(u^n_{i+1,j}+u^n_{i-1,j}+u^n_{i,j+1}+u^n_{i,j-1}-4u^n_{i,j}\right).

Step 3. Name the combination r=αΔth2r=\dfrac{\alpha\,\Delta t}{h^2}; it is the dimensionless αt/L2\alpha t/L^2 of Task 1.2 with time Δt\Delta t and length hh: ui,jn+1=ui,jn+r(ui+1,jn+ui−1,jn+ui,j+1n+ui,j−1n−4ui,jn)\boxed{\,u^{n+1}_{i,j}=u^n_{i,j}+r\left(u^n_{i+1,j}+u^n_{i-1,j}+u^n_{i,j+1}+u^n_{i,j-1}-4u^n_{i,j}\right)\,} In our program this is the function update_block in src/heat.cpp: out[i] = mid[i] + r * ((mid[i + 1] + mid[i - 1]) + (up[i] + down[i]) - 4.0 * mid[i]); (mid = row j, up/down = rows j ± 1). For Δx≠Δy\Delta x\neq\Delta y the xx-neighbours carry rx=αΔt/Δx2r_x=\alpha\Delta t/\Delta x^2 and the yy-neighbours ry=αΔt/Δy2r_y=\alpha\Delta t/\Delta y^2.

(a) General spacings, written out. un+1i,j = uni,j + rx (uni+1,j − 2uni,j + uni−1,j) + ry (uni,j+1 − 2uni,j + uni,j−1), with rx = αΔt/Δx² and ry = αΔt/Δy². With Δx = Δy = h both equal r and this becomes the boxed formula (part b).

(c) Space-time stencil. One value at level n+1n+1 needs five values at level nn: the centre and its four axis neighbours.

(d) Cost. Count the operations as the code writes them: 3 additions (sum of the four neighbours), 1 multiplication (4u4u), 1 subtraction, 1 multiplication by rr and 1 addition, so 3+1+1+1+1=73+1+1+1+1=7 flops, with 5 values read and 1 written. In practice the neighbours in the rows above and below are still in cache, so each node costs about 24 bytes of memory traffic (a read, a write and the write-allocate read, 8 bytes each): 7flop24byte≈0.3flop/byte.\frac{7\ \text{flop}}{24\ \text{byte}}\approx0.3\ \text{flop/byte}. That is why the loop is limited by memory bandwidth, not by arithmetic.

Task 5.3 (the same formula regrouped). ui,jn+1=(1−4r)ui,jn+r(ui−1,jn+ui+1,jn+ui,j−1n+ui,j+1n).u^{n+1}_{i,j}=(1-4r)\,u^n_{i,j}+r\left(u^n_{i-1,j}+u^n_{i+1,j}+u^n_{i,j-1}+u^n_{i,j+1}\right). The weights satisfy (1−4r)+4r=1(1-4r)+4r=1, so a uniform plate stays uniform.

Task 5.5: accuracy of the scheme

Step 1. Write the scheme as ui,jn+1−ui,jnΔt−α∇h2ui,jn=0\frac{u^{n+1}_{i,j}-u^n_{i,j}}{\Delta t}-\alpha\nabla_h^2u^n_{i,j}=0 and insert the exact solution.

Step 2 (time part). From the Taylor expansion in tt: u(t+Δt)−u(t)Δt=ut+Δt2utt+𝒪(Δt2).\frac{u(t+\Delta t)-u(t)}{\Delta t}=u_t+\frac{\Delta t}{2}u_{tt}+\mathcal O(\Delta t^2).

Step 3 (space part, Task 3.4). ∇h2u=∇2u+h212(uxxxx+uyyyy)+𝒪(h4).\nabla_h^2u=\nabla^2u+\frac{h^2}{12}\left(u_{xxxx}+u_{yyyy}\right)+\mathcal O(h^4).

Step 4. Subtract, and use the PDE ut=α∇2uu_t=\alpha\nabla^2u so that the leading terms cancel: τ=Δt2utt−αh212(uxxxx+uyyyy)+…\tau=\frac{\Delta t}{2}u_{tt}-\frac{\alpha h^2}{12}\left(u_{xxxx}+u_{yyyy}\right)+\dots τ=𝒪(Δtp)+𝒪(Δxq)+𝒪(Δyq),p=1,q=2\boxed{\,\tau=\mathcal O(\Delta t^{p})+\mathcal O(\Delta x^{q})+\mathcal O(\Delta y^{q}),\qquad p=1,\ q=2\,} It tends to 00 as the steps shrink, so the scheme is consistent.

A useful extra for test V. For u=sin⁡(πx)sin⁡(πy)e−2π2tu=\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\,e^{-2\pi^2t} we have utt=4π4uu_{tt}=4\pi^4u and uxxxx=uyyyy=π4uu_{xxxx}=u_{yyyy}=\pi^4u. With α=1\alpha=1 and Δt=rh2\Delta t=rh^2: τ=rh22(4π4u)−h212(2π4u)=π4h2(2r−16)u.\tau=\frac{rh^2}{2}\,(4\pi^4u)-\frac{h^2}{12}\,(2\pi^4u)=\pi^4h^2\left(2r-\frac16\right)u. This vanishes at r = 1/12: the two leading error terms cancel and the scheme becomes fourth order for this problem. Check with our program (python3 scripts/convergence.py 0.0833333333333): pobs = 3.54, 4.46, 3.54, 4.46 — about 4 on average, much better than 2. It is not exactly 4.000 because with T = 0.05 the number of steps T/(r h²) = 0.6 N² is not a whole number, so the program rounds it up and the r actually used is slightly below 1/12 (by a different amount on each grid).

Task 6.3: why r≤1/4r\le 1/4

Step 1. Take the scheme uj,kn+1=uj,kn+r(uj−1,kn+uj+1,kn+uj,k−1n+uj,k+1n−4uj,kn)u^{n+1}_{j,k}=u^n_{j,k}+r\left(u^n_{j-1,k}+u^n_{j+1,k}+u^n_{j,k-1}+u^n_{j,k+1}-4u^n_{j,k}\right) and insert the wave uj,kn=Gnei(jθ+kφ)u^n_{j,k}=G^n e^{\mathrm i(j\theta+k\varphi)}.

Indices: as in the notes, here j counts along x and k along y (the letter i is kept for the imaginary unit), so uj,k here is ui,j everywhere else in this document and in our code.

Step 2. Shifts become multiplications: uj±1,k=e±iθuj,ku_{j\pm1,k}=e^{\pm\mathrm i\theta}u_{j,k} and uj,k±1=e±iφuj,ku_{j,k\pm1}=e^{\pm\mathrm i\varphi}u_{j,k}. The new level is uj,kn+1=Guj,knu^{n+1}_{j,k}=G\,u^n_{j,k}.

Step 3. Divide everything by Gnei(jθ+kφ)G^ne^{\mathrm i(j\theta+k\varphi)}: G=1+r[(eiθ−2+e−iθ)+(eiφ−2+e−iφ)].G=1+r\left[\left(e^{\mathrm i\theta}-2+e^{-\mathrm i\theta}\right)+\left(e^{\mathrm i\varphi}-2+e^{-\mathrm i\varphi}\right)\right].

Step 4. Use identity (2.1), eiθ−2+e−iθ=−4sin⁡2θ2,e^{\mathrm i\theta}-2+e^{-\mathrm i\theta}=-4\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}, which follows from eiθ+e−iθ=2cos⁡θe^{\mathrm i\theta}+e^{-\mathrm i\theta}=2\cos\theta and 1−cos⁡θ=2sin⁡2(θ/2)1-\cos\theta=2\sin^2(\theta/2). Then G(θ,φ)=1−4r[sin⁡2θ2+sin⁡2φ2]\boxed{\,G(\theta,\varphi)=1-4r\left[\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}+\sin^2\frac{\varphi}{2}\right]\,} GG is real.

Step 5. Each sin⁡2\sin^2 lies in [0,1][0,1], so 1−8r≤G≤11-8r\le G\le1. The upper bound always holds. The smallest value is at θ=φ=π\theta=\varphi=\pi, the checkerboard (−1)j+k(-1)^{j+k}, where both sines equal 11 and G=1−8rG=1-8r.

Step 6. Stability needs |G|≤1\lvert G\rvert\le1 for every wave, so 1−8r≥−1⇔r≤141-8r\ge-1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\boxed{\,r\le\frac14\,} For general spacings the condition is rx+ry≤12r_x+r_y\le\frac12. In terms of the time step, this is (part b) Δt ≤ 1 / (2α (1/Δx² + 1/Δy²)); with Δx = Δy = h (part c): Δt ≤ h²/(4α), i.e. r ≤ 1/4. In our program, src/main.cpp refuses to run when r > 1/4 and explains why (only --force-unstable, used for Task 6.7, bypasses it).

(d) Other dimensions. In 1D there is one sine, G=1−4rsin⁡2(θ/2)G=1-4r\sin^2(\theta/2), with worst case 1−4r≥−11-4r\ge-1, so r≤12r\le\frac12. In 3D there are three sines, worst case 1−12r≥−11-12r\ge-1, so r≤16r\le\frac16.

Task 8.2: strips against blocks

Let tst_s be the start-up time of a message, twt_w the time per double and tct_c the time of one node update.

(a) Strips. Each process owns N/PN/P full rows and needs the row above and the row below. It sends 2 rows and receives 2, each of about NN doubles: 2 messages each way.

(b) Blocks. Each process owns a square of side N/PN/\sqrt P with 4 sides of N/PN/\sqrt P doubles each: 4 messages each way, 4N/P4N/\sqrt P doubles in total.

(c) Time per step (one message time per exchange, since inside MPI_Sendrecv the send and the receive overlap): Tstrips=2(ts+Ntw),Tblocks=4(ts+NPtw).T_{\text{strips}}=2\left(t_s+N\,t_w\right),\qquad T_{\text{blocks}}=4\left(t_s+\frac{N}{\sqrt P}\,t_w\right).

When are blocks faster? Require Tblocks<TstripsT_{\text{blocks}}<T_{\text{strips}}: 4ts+4NtwP<2ts+2Ntw⇔2ts<Ntw(2−4P).4t_s+\frac{4N\,t_w}{\sqrt P}<2t_s+2N\,t_w\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2t_s<N\,t_w\left(2-\frac{4}{\sqrt P}\right). This can only hold if 2−4/P>02-4/\sqrt P>0, i.e. P>4P>4, and then N>N*=tstw(1−2P)\boxed{\,N>N^\ast=\frac{t_s}{t_w\left(1-\dfrac{2}{\sqrt P}\right)}\,}

  • At P=4P=4 the data volumes are equal (4/4=24/\sqrt4=2), so blocks only add latency and never win.
  • Small tst_s: N*N^\ast is small and blocks win for P>4P>4 (less data).
  • Large tst_s: N*N^\ast is huge and strips win (fewer messages).

With our measured network (Step 5, ping-pong between the two VMs over Tailscale, results/raw/pingpong_laetitia_georges.csv): ts ≈ 29.0 ms and 6.7 µs per byte (0.15 MB/s), so tw ≈ 5.4 × 10⁻⁵ s per double. Then N* = ts / (tw(1 − 2/√P)) gives: P = 4: blocks never win; P = 8: N* ≈ 1850; P = 16: N* ≈ 1080. Communication time per step from the formulas in (c):

Our network has a huge latency but also a very low bandwidth, so the data volume matters: once P > 4, blocks send less data and win from N ≈ 1850. With 2 VMs × 2 cores we can only run P ≤ 4, where strips should always be (slightly) faster — this is what the performance runs will check. These numbers count only the messages between the two VMs; messages between two processes on the same VM are thousands of times cheaper (0.014 ms, 2.5 GB/s).

(d) Communication-to-computation ratio for strips. Computation per step is tcN2/Pt_c\,N^2/P, so TcommTcomp=2(ts+Ntw)tcN2/P=2PtstcN2+2PtwtcN.\frac{T_{\text{comm}}}{T_{\text{comp}}}=\frac{2\left(t_s+N\,t_w\right)}{t_c\,N^2/P}=\frac{2P\,t_s}{t_c\,N^2}+\frac{2P\,t_w}{t_c\,N}. With PP fixed this falls like 1/N1/N (and the latency term like 1/N21/N^2): computation grows like the area, communication like the perimeter. Parallelism over a slow link pays off only for large NN. With our numbers (ts = 29 ms, tw = 5.4 × 10⁻⁵ s per double, and tc ≈ 1.75 ns per node update measured in Step 7, i.e. 0.57 × 10⁹ updates/s), for P = 4: the latency term is small only if N ≫ √(2P ts/tc) ≈ 11 500, and the bandwidth term only if N ≫ 2P tw/tc ≈ 245 000. At our largest grid, N = 8192, the ratio is ≈ 2 + 30 = 32: the processes would spend about 32 times longer communicating than computing. Prediction: running across our two VMs is slower than one VM for every N we can test; spreading over machines would only pay off with a much faster network (on a real cluster with ts ≈ 1 µs the latency limit drops to N ≫ about 70).
